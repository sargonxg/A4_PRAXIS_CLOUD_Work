Sahel Q2 2026 — Mid-Cycle Review

JNIM consolidation in northern Mali is converging with a slow-motion ECOWAS realignment. The negotiating window through stage 6 is narrower than usual, but it has not yet closed.

Author · PRAXIS Daily Brief Calibrated for · Senior Conflict Analyst, Foreign Policy Desk Generated · 2026-04-28 13:42 UTC Profile · v7 Reading time · 36 min

Executive summary

The Mali Northern Insurgency moved another half-step on the Glasl scale during April. JNIM communiqués this month are no longer purely defensive; they now articulate a positive territorial program for the Liptako-Gourma corridor, and a thin but consistent line of internal succession discussion has appeared across three independent intercepts.[S-217] The Algiers Process is stalled but not collapsed; Algerian foreign-ministry signaling on 25 April was the strongest pro-mediation gesture in seven months.[S-219]

Risk composite stands at 7.3 (medium-high), up +0.4 from the seven-day average. INFORM v2.1 places the situation inside its "ripeness" band per Zartman, which historically opens a 60–90 day track-II window before the next escalation step.

"De-escalation through stage 6 is still viable, but the cost of waiting compounds linearly after week three." — Risk engine summary, calibrated to mandate conflict-prevention

What's at stake

Three things that matter, in priority order for a foreign-policy desk: (1) the survival of a credible mediation framework anchored to the Algiers process; (2) the durability of the ECOWAS sanctions architecture under intra-bloc pressure; and (3) the integrity of UN MINUSMA's verification mandate as it transitions to a leaner mission.

Actor configuration

Inside JNIM, a quiet succession discussion has begun. Three internal communiqués this month reference a shura rotation that would, if implemented, move operational authority away from the Iyad Ag Ghaly faction toward a younger mid-tier cadre with stronger ties to the Macina front. PRAXIS has not yet confirmed this shift; it is currently rated Inferred, not Extracted.[S-221]

On the state side, FAMa command has produced two operational orders that — read together — suggest preparation for a defensive posture along the 16N line rather than a renewed offensive into Kidal. This is a more meaningful tell than any public statement.[S-225]

Three scenarios

The three plausible trajectories below are weighted by independent probability per the Fearon-rationalist branch of the scenario engine. Weights sum to 92%; the residual 8% is allocated to "tail" trajectories not detailed here.

Scenario A — Algiers reactivation (32%)

Algeria reopens the mediation process with Mauritanian and Nigerien observer states, taking advantage of the ECOWAS opening. JNIM's mid-tier cadre signals contingent willingness through Mauritanian channels. Implication: a 45-day track-II window opens; risk composite falls toward 6.4 by June.

Scenario B — Status-quo erosion (38%)

Neither side moves. ECOWAS sanctions hold, Algiers stays stalled. JNIM advances along the corridor without serious territorial overreach. Implication: risk drifts to 7.6–7.9, stage 6 reached late summer.

Scenario C — Externalization (22%)

JNIM-ISGS rivalry tips into an external operation aimed at coastal states (Togo, Benin), forcing ECOWAS into a security-first posture and freezing diplomatic initiatives. Implication: risk composite jumps to 8.5+ within 30 days; stage 7 dynamics become live.

Policy implications

For a desk operating with a conflict-prevention mandate, three near-term moves matter most:

  1. Quietly reinforce Algerian mediation capacity — bilateral, not multilateral; multilateral framing has consistently triggered JNIM hardening.
  2. Pre-position humanitarian access agreements with both Mali and Burkina, decoupled from the mediation track, to reduce escalation friction in Scenario B.
  3. Prepare a rapid sanctions-relief signaling package contingent on a Stage 5 → 4 step-down — rewarding the direction of motion rather than waiting for full reversal.
