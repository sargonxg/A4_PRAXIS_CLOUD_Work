# The portal and the paid deliverable

The route is `/signal` inside PRAXIS, inheriting its login, navigation, design system and authorization. These screens are production requirements. The included HTML reference is a limited demonstration.

## Desk home

The first screen answers "What has changed for this question?" A country/region selector is followed by the current mandate and horizon. Present the latest reviewed assessment, its change reason, material contrary evidence and unresolved questions. A compact status strip shows panel revision, included languages, collection cutoff, receipt freshness and missing/degraded sources. Empty coverage is an actionable configuration state.

Below it, list narrative candidates and reviewed narratives with attributed wording, relevant actor/speaker, qualifying stance, distinct source-family counts and comparison window. Every change statistic shows numerator, denominator and panel/window comparability. Selecting one opens its original-language evidence and review state. Source attention is not public opinion or audience reach.

## Evidence and review

Use a split view: original source representation with highlighted exact locator on one side, structured claim/frame/stance proposal and qualification on the other. Show original date, capture date, hash/revision, rights status and translation lineage. Keep negation, modal language and the difference between speaker and event actor visible. Provide accept, reject and unresolved decisions with rationale and scope of review.

For multilingual material, place original and translation adjacent without implying translation is the canonical source. Isolate mixed-direction inline content and identifiers so Arabic/Hebrew passages remain readable. W3C provides practical markup guidance for bidirectional inline text. Apply it in the current component framework and test keyboard, screen-reader and copy/paste behavior. [R18]

## Graph and dossier

Open the graph from an analytical question rather than a decorative wall of nodes. Filter by predicate and candidate/reviewed status, limit expansion, and show the source receipt for a selected edge. Provide the same information as a keyboard-accessible list. A narrative dossier shows membership history, aliases, language variants, observed quotation/reuse relations, competing frames and rejected interpretations.

## Reviewable brief

An export has an audience/mandate, date and evidence cutoff, scope/panel statement, author/reviewer, status and revision. Its recommended structure is:

1. Key judgment and why it matters to the stated question.
2. What changed since the previous approved brief.
3. Attributed claims and competing interpretations, with original locators.
4. Material dissent, alternative explanation and unknowns.
5. Decision implications expressed within the customer's mandate.
6. Indicators to watch, next review trigger and collection limits.
7. Pinned source/dependency manifest and review receipt.

Generate a draft only from permitted pinned evidence and reviewed context. Require editorial approval before the final status. Export Markdown/PDF for reading and a structured evidence bundle for reuse in PRAXIS; each respects source rights. Restricted source content may need a link/excerpt or redacted receipt rather than full text. A beautiful PDF without traceable evidence is not the acceptance target.

## Demonstration acceptance

Use a synthetic policy-announcement scenario, then a permissioned real desk. Show two languages and one contrary source; review a claim; approve a brief; introduce a correction; inspect affected graph/capsule dependencies; issue a delta. Repeat in another country and a regional scope. The demonstration should show how the analytical answer changes and allow a buyer to verify it. Real buyer demand and analytical accuracy remain to be measured.
